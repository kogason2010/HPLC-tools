Equations used
  • Flow: F2 = F1 · (dc2²/dc1²) · (dp1/dp2) — the dp term is optional.
  • Injection volume: V2 = V1 · (L2 dc2²)/(L1 dc1²)
  • Gradient times: t2 = t1 · (F1/F2) · (L2 dc2²)/(L1 dc1²)
  • Pressure ratio (same mobile phase): P2/P1 = (F2/F1)(L2/L1)(dc1/dc2)²(dp1/dp2)²
  • Dwell: the original dwell volume, scaled to the new column volume, is compared with the new system's dwell volume. A shortfall is made up with an initial isocratic hold; an excess can be offset by delaying injection, if the system supports it.
  • USP <621> (harmonized, official Dec 2022): L/dp may change by −25% to +50% for isocratic and gradient methods. Always confirm against the current official chapter and verify system suitability.

Equations & limits
  • ΔP = φ · η · L · u / dp², with u = F/(π dc²/4) (superficial velocity). φ ≈ 1000 is typical for packed beds; real columns vary by roughly ±30%, so treat the result as an estimate and calibrate φ against a measured pressure if you can.
  • Viscosity: empirical fit of ln η against volume fraction and 1/T for each binary mixture. It reproduces pure water, methanol and acetonitrile viscosities and the methanol–water maximum (≈1.6 cP near 40–50% at 25 °C) reasonably well; treat mid-range values as approximate (±10–15%). It ignores buffer salts and additives.
  • System pressure (tubing, mixer, detector) is not included.
